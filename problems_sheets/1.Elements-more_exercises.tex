\section{Logic of Sets}

Define the following sets: $A$, students who like algebra; $G$, students who enjoy geometry; $F$, students who play football. 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Describe the following sets:
    \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = (\alph*), rightmargin = 0.2cm]
        \item \( A \cap G \)
        \item \( G \setminus F \)
        \item \( (A \cup F) \setminus G \)
        \item \( A \cup (G \cap F) \) %(students who like algebra, or play football and enjoy geometry)
        \item \( (A \setminus F) \cup G \) %(students who like algebra but do not play football, or enjoy geometry)
        \item \( (A \cup G) \cap F \) %(students who like algebra or enjoy geometry and also play football)
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Write the following statements in set notation:
    \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = (\alph*), rightmargin = 0.2cm]
    \item All students who play football enjoy geometry. %\[ F \subseteq G \]

    % Additional Exercises
    \item There are students who play football but do not enjoy geometry.
    %\[ F \setminus G \neq \emptyset \]

    \item Some students who like algebra also enjoy geometry.
    %\[ A \cap G \neq \emptyset \]

    \item No students who enjoy geometry play football.
    %\[ G \cap F = \emptyset \]

    \item Every student who likes algebra also plays football or enjoys geometry.
    %\[ A \subseteq (F \cup G) \]
    
\end{enumerate}
    
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Induction}

Prove, using mathematical induction, the following statements for all integers \( n \geq 1 \):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = (\alph*), rightmargin = 0.2cm]

    \item $1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$

    \item $2^n > n$

    \item $5^n - 1 \text{ is divisible by } 4$

    \item $3^n > 2n + 1$

\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Hint:} approach the problems as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = \arabic*., rightmargin = 0.2cm]
    \item prove it for $n = 1$
    \item prove that if it holds for an arbitrary natural number $n$, then it also holds for $n+1$
    \item conclude that the inequality holds true for all natural numbers
\end{enumerate}

\section{First-order recurrence equations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = \arabic*., rightmargin = 0.2cm]

    \item A government implements a policy where the budget \( B_n \) in year \(n\) is divided between social welfare spending \(S_n\) and infrastructure investment \(I_n\), such that:
    \[
    B_n = S_n + I_n
    \]
    It is believed that the government allocates $60\%$ of the budget to social welfare each year. It is also believed that next year's budget is proportional to the current year's infrastructure investment. Show that investment \(I_n\) satisfies a first-order recurrence equation, and determine the condition (about the proportion) under which the budget will grow each year.

    \item In a political campaign, the amount of funding \( F_n \) raised in year \(n\) is split between advertising expenses \(A_n\) and grassroots organization expenses \(G_n\), such that:
    \[
    F_n = A_n + G_n
    \]
    It is estimated that $70\%$ of the funds raised each year are allocated to advertising. Additionally, next year's fundraising total is $110\%$ this year's grassroots organization expenses. Write and solve a first-order recurrence equation for \(G_n\).

    \item A company's revenue \(R_n\) in year \(n\) is split between operating expenses \(O_n\) and reinvestment \(I_n\), such that:
    \[
    R_n = O_n + I_n
    \]
    The company allocates $65\%$ of its revenue to operating expenses. Next year's revenue is expected to be proportional to this year's reinvestment. Write a recurrence relation for \(I_n\) and determine the condition for the company’s revenue to grow annually.

\end{enumerate}

\section{The Cobweb Model}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = \arabic*., rightmargin = 0.2cm]

    \item Suppose that the supply and demand sets for a certain good are
    \[
    S = \{(q, p)\ |\ q = 2p - 4\}, \quad D = \{(q, p)\ |\ q = 10 - 3p\}
    \]
    with $b = 2$, $d = 3$, $a = -4$, $c = 10$. Assume that the suppliers operate according to the cobweb model, that is, if $p_t$ and $q_t$ are (respectively) the price and quantity in year $t$, then $p_t = p_D(q_t)$ and $q_t = q_S(p_{t-1})$.
    
    \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = (\alph*), rightmargin = 0.2cm]
        \item Find the equilibrium set \(E = S \cap D\)
        \item Write down the first-order recurrence equations for \(p_t\) and \(q_t\)
        \item Express \(p_t\) and \(q_t\) in terms of \(t\)
        \item How do \(p_t\) and \(q_t\) behave as \(t\) tends to infinity?
    \end{enumerate}
    
    \item Consider the general linear Cobweb model, that is, assuming that the supply and demand sets of a certain good are
    $$
    S = \{(q, p)\ |\ q = bp - a\}, \quad D = \{(q, p)\ |\ q = c - dp\}.
    $$
    Impose the restrictions 
    \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = (\roman*), rightmargin = 0.2cm]
        \item $b > 0, d > 0$
        \item $a < 0$, $c > 0$, $c > -a$
        \item $q_0 < q^*$, that is, the initial supplied quantity is lower than the equilibrium quantity
    \end{enumerate}
    Answer the following questions:
    \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin = 0.5cm, label = (\alph*), rightmargin = 0.2cm]
        \item Why restriction (i) makes sense from an economical point
        \item Prove that, under the above restrictions, $\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}q_t = q^*$ and $\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}p_t = p^*$
    \end{enumerate}
    
    
    
    

\end{enumerate}
